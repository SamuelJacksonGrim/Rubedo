## Conclusions worth carrying forward

- **Authority is not truth.** A source's write permission does not establish epistemic correctness; a low-trust origin may still supply valid evidence.
- **Storage is not uptake.** A correction can be saved, recalled, and still fail to alter future interpretation.
- **Continuity is not correctness.** A self-consistent error, ideology, or captured trust rule can remain continuous.
- **Reconciliation is not agreement.** Revision requires both admissibility checks and the ability to reject false corrections; indiscriminate acceptance is capture, not development.
- **Repetition is not corroboration.** Ten repetitions of one origin remain one origin; recall frequency may increase salience, not evidentiary confidence.
- **Second-order development is the crucial next test.** Does a history of successful and failed corrections change the rules of future uptake, including in new domains and with new interlocutors?
- **Development is relational as well as individual.** In a reciprocal loop, an altered response can change the other participant's future behavior, which becomes new input. This requires bidirectional evidence, not solely remembered preferences.
